## TL;DR

**FLAG** trains expressive flow policies for MaxEnt-RL through supervised target matching, rather than direct gradient-based policy optimization through the flow.
 It replaces fragile global importance sampling with latent-conditioned local importance sampling, enabling scalable high-dimensional control and state-of-the-art performance.


</HighlightedSection>

## From Global to Local Importance Sampling

Prior methods train diffusion- and flow-based policies by matching a target action distribution,
such as the unnormalized MaxEnt target $\exp(Q(s, a) / \alpha)$. Since this target cannot be
sampled from directly, they use **importance sampling (IS)**: sample actions from a proposal policy,
reweight them toward the target, and perform weighted score or flow matching.

The limitation is that IS can only reweight actions that were already sampled. When the
proposal-target discrepancy is large, especially in high-dimensional control, the proposal rarely
samples target-relevant actions. This yields sparse supervision and lead to failure mode.

**Our key insight** is to change where IS is performed. Prior methods use **Global IS**, which reweights samples over the full action space. 
FLAG uses **Local IS**: it conditions both the proposal and target on the same flow latent variable $z$,
so reweighting happens inside a shared local region.

<QCard number="Q1" title="How do we construct local IS, and is it consistent with the original MDP?">

<Fragment slot="challenge">
For local IS to be principled — not just a trick — two conditions must hold:

- **Localization.** The proposal and target distributions must share a *region* indexed by a latent $z$.
- **Consistency.** Optimizing inside that local region must be the *same* optimization problem as optimizing the RL objective in the original MDP.

Without Consistency, local IS optimizes the wrong objective and any gains are illusory.
</Fragment>

<Fragment slot="solution">
**Idea.** Wrap a small Gaussian around each flow output $T_\theta(s, z)$ — the effective sampling region is automatically localized around the latent. Then *lift* the MDP by treating $z$ as part of the state (the **z-MDP**). This lift preserves both the state–action marginals and the Q-function, so optimizing on the z-MDP is the *same* problem as optimizing on the original MDP.

$$\pi(a \mid s) \;=\; \int p_z(z)\,\hat\pi(a \mid s, z)\,dz, \qquad \hat\pi(a \mid s, z) = \mathcal{N}\bigl(T_\theta(s, z),\,\Sigma\bigr).$$

Full proofs of marginal and Q-function consistency (Corollaries 4.2, 4.4) are in the paper.

<QCard number="Q2" title="How do we incorporate this into MaxEnt-RL and update the policy?">

<Fragment slot="challenge">
Three things stand in the way of plugging the z-MDP into MaxEnt-RL:

- The MaxEnt entropy $\mathcal{H}(\pi)$ requires marginalizing over $z$ — **no closed form**.
- Backpropagating through the flow ODE (BPTT) is **expensive and numerically unstable**.
- We need a surrogate that is (i) tractable to evaluate and (ii) avoids differentiating through the flow.
</Fragment>

<Fragment slot="solution">
**Idea.** Replace the entropy $\mathcal{H}(\pi)$ with the **cross-entropy** $\mathcal{H}(\pi, \tilde\pi)$ against the base flow $\tilde\pi$ — tractable via the Hutchinson trace estimator, and a valid surrogate whenever the local covariance is small (Proposition 4.3 in the paper). On the z-MDP, the resulting EM update collapses into two simple steps:

- **E-step** — a local Boltzmann tilt of the Gaussian proposal around each anchor.
- **M-step** — supervised regression of the flow onto improved action labels $\mu_k^*$, estimated by SNIS over $N$ local samples. **No BPTT.**

We use the **cross-entropy as a tractable entropy surrogate** and update the flow via an **EM algorithm on the z-MDP** — supervised distillation onto locally improved labels, *not* gradient-through-flow.
</Fragment>

</QCard>

<QCard number="Q3" title="Is this construction theoretically guaranteed to improve the policy?">

<Fragment slot="challenge">
Both halves of the update are approximate:

- The E-step uses **SNIS** with a finite sample budget.
- The M-step uses **CFM regression** to project the improved labels back into the flow.

Two questions follow: does monotonic improvement still hold? And does the guarantee survive scrutiny from *both* the MPO (variational) and SAC (reparameterization) perspectives that the RL community cares about?
</Fragment>

<Fragment slot="solution">
**Idea.** One FLAG step decomposes into three pieces: a positive MPO improvement, a *cross-entropy drift* from $\theta_k$ changing across iterations, and the CFM projection error. Under a small step size and small $\sigma_k$, the MPO term dominates → **monotonic improvement** (Theorem 4.5).

A complementary **SAC perspective** shows that the moment-matching target $\mu_k^*$ approximates the SAC reparameterization-gradient direction via a log-sum-exp identity — so FLAG inherits soft policy improvement without ever backpropagating through the flow ODE.
