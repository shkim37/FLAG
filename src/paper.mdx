## TL;DR

**FLAG** trains expressive flow policies for MaxEnt-RL through supervised target matching, rather than direct gradient-based policy optimization through the flow.
 It replaces fragile global importance sampling with latent-conditioned local importance sampling, enabling scalable high-dimensional control and state-of-the-art performance.


</HighlightedSection>

## From Global to Local Importance Sampling

Prior methods train diffusion- and flow-based policies by matching a target action distribution,
such as the unnormalized MaxEnt target $\exp(Q(s, a) / \alpha)$. Since this target cannot be
sampled from directly, they use **importance sampling (IS)**: sample actions from a proposal policy,
reweight them toward the target, and perform weighted score or flow matching.

The limitation is that IS can only reweight actions that were already sampled. When the
proposal-target discrepancy is large, especially in high-dimensional control, the proposal rarely
samples target-relevant actions. This yields sparse supervision and lead to failure mode.

**Our key insight** is to change where IS is performed. Prior methods use **Global IS**, which reweights samples over the full action space. 
FLAG uses **Local IS**: it conditions both the proposal and target on the same flow latent variable $z$,
so reweighting happens inside a shared local region.

## Q1. How do we construct local IS — and is it consistent with the original MDP?

**Challenge.** Local IS needs (i) a *localized* proposal that stays informative under a small sample budget, and (ii) a guarantee that optimizing in this localized space is equivalent to optimizing in the original MDP. Without (ii), localization buys nothing.

**Key idea.** Wrap a small Gaussian around each flow anchor $T_\theta(s, z)$ so the effective sampling region is localized around the latent $z$. Then *lift* the MDP by treating $z$ as part of the state; we show this lift preserves both state-action marginals and Q-values.

**Local policy.** A flow map $T_\theta(s, z)$ transports a latent $z \sim p_z$ to an anchor action. We attach an isotropic Gaussian to this anchor:

$$
\hat\pi(a \mid s, z;\,\theta) = \mathcal{N}\bigl(a;\, T_\theta(s,z),\, \Sigma\bigr),
\qquad
\pi(a \mid s;\,\theta) = \int p_z(z)\,\hat\pi(a \mid s, z;\,\theta)\,dz.
$$

**Latent-augmented MDP ($z$-MDP).** Append $z$ to the state, $\hat s = (s, z)$:

$$
\hat p(\hat s' \mid \hat s, a) = p(s' \mid s, a)\,p_z(z'), \qquad \hat r(\hat s, a) = r(s, a).
$$

**Consistency.** Two corollaries pin down the equivalence between $z$-MDP and the original MDP. *Marginal consistency* states that $\mathbb E_{\hat\pi}[f(s, a)] = \mathbb E_\pi[f(s, a)]$ for any measurable $f$ (expectations are taken under the discounted state-action marginal), and *Q-function consistency* states that $Q^{\hat\pi}(\hat s, a) = Q^{\pi}(s, a)$ for all $s, z, a$.

{/* [PLACEHOLDER FIGURE: z-MDP lift diagram — original MDP ↔ z-MDP] */}

<ColorBox color="#0F766E" title="Takeaway">
Optimizing $Q^{\pi}(s, a)$ via the local policy $\hat\pi$ over $\hat s \sim \hat\rho_{\hat\pi}$ is **equivalent** to optimizing the global policy $\pi$ over $s \sim \rho_\pi$. Local IS comes for free from this lift.
</ColorBox>


## Q2. How do we incorporate this into MaxEnt-RL and update the policy?

**Challenge.** The MaxEnt-RL objective requires $\mathcal{H}(\pi)$, but $\pi$ marginalizes $z$ with no closed form. Differentiating through the flow (BPTT) is expensive and unstable.

**Key idea.** Replace $\mathcal{H}(\pi)$ with the **cross-entropy** $\mathcal{H}(\pi, \tilde\pi)$ against the base flow policy $\tilde\pi$ — tractable via the Hutchinson trace estimator, and a valid surrogate when $\Sigma$ is small. Plugged into the $z$-MDP, the EM update collapses to a supervised regression on the flow.

**Cross-entropy as a valid surrogate.** For smooth $\tilde\pi$ and small $\mathrm{tr}(\Sigma)$,

$$
\mathcal{H}(\pi, \tilde\pi) = \mathcal{H}(\pi) + \mathcal{O}\bigl(\mathrm{tr}(\Sigma)^2\bigr).
$$

This yields a tractable proxy MaxEnt-RL objective evaluated in the $z$-MDP.

**EM update.** Treating $\tilde\pi_{\theta_k}$ as a fixed reference, define the energy $f_{\hat s, k}(a) := Q^{\pi_k}(s, a) - \alpha \log \tilde\pi_{\theta_k}(a \mid s)$. Then for each $\hat s$:

$$
\text{(E-step)}\quad q_k(a \mid \hat s) \;\propto\; \hat\pi(a \mid \hat s;\,\theta_k)\,\exp\bigl(f_{\hat s, k}(a)/\lambda\bigr),
$$

$$
\text{(M-step)}\quad \theta_{k+1} \;\in\; \arg\min_\theta\; \mathbb E_{(s, z) \sim \hat\rho_{\hat\pi}}\;\| T_\theta(s, z) - \mu_k^*(\hat s) \|^2,
\quad \mu_k^*(\hat s) := \mathbb E_{q_k}[a].
$$

The M-step is **moment matching**: $\mu_k^*$ is estimated via self-normalized IS on $N$ Gaussian samples and distilled back into the flow with the CFM objective. The label $\mu_k^*$ acts as a *guiding flag* telling each latent $z$ where to generate.

{/* [PLACEHOLDER FIGURE: fig:4.3 — local moment-matching + distillation diagram] */}

<ColorBox color="#0F766E" title="Takeaway">
The flow is updated by **supervised regression onto locally improved action labels** — no BPTT through the flow, and no global proposal degeneracy.
</ColorBox>


## Q3. Is this construction theoretically guaranteed to improve the policy?

**Challenge.** Approximate E-step (SNIS) plus approximate M-step (CFM regression) sounds heuristic. Does monotonic improvement still hold?

**Key idea.** Decompose one EM step into three terms: the standard frozen-reward MPO improvement, a drift from the cross-entropy reward as $\theta_k$ changes, and the CFM projection error. The first dominates under mild conditions on $\sigma_k$ and projection error.

**Monotonic improvement.** For sufficiently small step size $\beta$,

$$
\mathcal{J}_{k+1} - \mathcal{J}_k
\;\geq\;
\underbrace{\lambda\beta\,\mathcal{G}_k}_{\text{frozen-reward MPO}}
\;-\;
\underbrace{\alpha\, C_\Sigma\, \sigma_k^2\, \beta\,\mathcal{G}_k}_{\text{cross-entropy drift}}
\;-\;
\underbrace{\lambda\,\epsilon_k^{\mathrm{proj}}}_{\text{CFM projection error}}
\;+\; \mathcal{O}(\beta^2).
$$

The guarantee holds whenever $\lambda > \alpha\,C_\Sigma\,\sigma_k^2$ and $\epsilon_k^{\mathrm{proj}}$ is small.

{/* [PLACEHOLDER FIGURE: fig:flag-mpo-flow — three-term decomposition / proof sketch] */}

<ColorBox color="#0F766E" title="Takeaway">
Monotonic improvement is conditional on **two practical knobs**: keeping $\sigma_k$ small, and keeping the CFM projection error small. Q4 shows how the implementation enforces both.
</ColorBox>


## Q4. Do the heuristic design choices satisfy the conditions in practice?

**Challenge.** Theorem in Q3 gives abstract sufficient conditions. We need (i) implementation choices that *map onto* those conditions, and (ii) empirical evidence that the resulting algorithm works.

**Each design choice targets a specific term.**

- *Covariance scheduling.* Anneal $\sigma_k$ from $\sigma_{\mathrm{init}}$ to a small $\sigma_{\mathrm{final}}$ — directly shrinks the $\alpha\,C_\Sigma\,\sigma_k^2$ drift term.
- *Effective temperature.* Fix a reference $\lambda_{\mathrm{ref}}$ and use $\lambda = \alpha\,\lambda_{\mathrm{ref}}$. This reduces the sufficient condition to $\lambda_{\mathrm{ref}} > C_\Sigma\,\sigma_k^2$ and avoids dual-Lagrangian instability between $\alpha$ and $\lambda$.
- *Guidance buffer.* Cache recent improved labels $\mu_k^*$ and reuse them across off-policy updates — provides dense supervision that keeps $\epsilon_k^{\mathrm{proj}}$ small in practice.
- *Autotuned $\alpha$.* Adapt the entropy scaling via $\mathcal{L}(\alpha) = \alpha\bigl(\mathcal{H}(\pi, \tilde\pi) - \mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{target}}\bigr)$.
