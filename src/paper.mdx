## TL;DR

**FLAG** trains expressive flow policies for MaxEnt-RL through supervised target matching, rather than direct gradient-based policy optimization through the flow.
 It replaces fragile global importance sampling with latent-conditioned local importance sampling, enabling scalable high-dimensional control and state-of-the-art performance.


</HighlightedSection>

## From Global to Local Importance Sampling

Prior methods train diffusion- and flow-based policies by matching a target action distribution,
such as the unnormalized MaxEnt target $\exp(Q(s, a) / \alpha)$. Since this target cannot be
sampled from directly, they use **importance sampling (IS)**: sample actions from a proposal policy,
reweight them toward the target, and perform weighted score or flow matching.

The limitation is that IS can only reweight actions that were already sampled. When the
proposal-target discrepancy is large, especially in high-dimensional control, the proposal rarely
samples target-relevant actions. This yields sparse supervision and lead to failure mode.

**Our key insight** is to change where IS is performed. Prior methods use **Global IS**, which reweights samples over the full action space. 
FLAG uses **Local IS**: it conditions both the proposal and target on the same flow latent variable $z$,
so reweighting happens inside a shared local region.

## Method

<MethodHint />

<QCard number="Q1" title="How do we construct local IS, and is it consistent with the original MDP?">

<Fragment slot="challenge">
For local IS to be principled — not just a trick — two conditions must hold:

- **Localization.** The proposal and target distributions must share a *region* indexed by a latent $z$.
- **Consistency.** Optimizing inside that local region must be the *same* optimization problem as optimizing the RL objective in the original MDP.

Without Consistency, local IS optimizes the wrong objective and any gains are illusory.
</Fragment>

<Fragment slot="solution">
<IdeaTabs labels={["Policy Parameterization", "Latent-Augmented MDP", "Consistency"]}>
  <Fragment slot="tab-0">
    <Figure>
      <Picture slot="figure" src="../assets/policy_parameterization.pdf" alt="Flow map transports latent z to multi-modal anchor actions, around which local Gaussians form the local policy and marginalize into the global policy" />
      <Fragment slot="caption">The deterministic flow map $T_\theta(s, z)$ transports a latent prior $z \sim p_z$ to multi-modal anchor actions. Wrapping each anchor with a small Gaussian gives a tractable *local* density $\hat\pi(a \mid s, z)$.</Fragment>
    </Figure>
  </Fragment>
  <Fragment slot="tab-1">
    <Figure>
      <Picture slot="figure" src="../assets/zMDP.pdf" alt="Graphical model of the latent-augmented MDP showing the local-policy path (red), the global-policy marginalization (blue), and the environment dynamics (black)" />
      <Fragment slot="caption">The **z-MDP** augments each state with the latent $z$, giving a separate local policy $\hat\pi(a \mid s, z)$ per region. The red path shows local anchor generation from $(s, z, \theta)$; the blue path marginalizes over $z$ to recover the global policy $\pi(a \mid s)$.</Fragment>
    </Figure>
  </Fragment>
  <Fragment slot="tab-2">
    The z-MDP preserves both **state–action marginals** and the **Q-function** — optimizing on the z-MDP is the *same* problem as on the original MDP. Local IS becomes principled, not a heuristic.

    <div class="not-prose my-3 grid gap-3 md:grid-cols-2">
      <EqBox label="Corollary 4.2 — Marginal consistency">
        $$
        \rho_\pi(s, a) = \int \hat\rho_{\hat\pi}(s, z, a)\,dz
        $$
      </EqBox>
      <EqBox label="Corollary 4.4 — Q-function consistency">
        $$
        Q^{\hat\pi}(\hat s, a) = Q^{\pi}(s, a), \quad \forall\, s, z, a
        $$
      </EqBox>
    </div>

    <small class="mt-2 block text-xs italic text-zinc-500 dark:text-zinc-400">Full statements (Corollaries 4.2, 4.4) are in the paper. Details are in Appendix B.</small>
  </Fragment>
</IdeaTabs>
</Fragment>

<Fragment slot="bottom-line">
We construct the **z-MDP**, and prove that optimizing the RL objective on the z-MDP is **equivalent** to optimizing it on the original MDP (**Consistency**). Local IS becomes principled, not a heuristic.
</Fragment>

</QCard>

<QCard number="Q2" title="How do we incorporate this into MaxEnt-RL and update the policy?">

<Fragment slot="challenge">
Two obstacles block plugging the z-MDP into MaxEnt-RL:

- **Intractable Entropy.** The composite policy's log-probability $\log\pi(a \mid s)$ requires marginalizing over the latent $z$ — there is **no closed form**.
- **RL via Supervised Learning.** We want to cast MaxEnt-RL as an **EM algorithm** whose policy update reduces to a *supervised learning* problem — avoiding backprop through the flow ODE (BPTT).
</Fragment>

<Fragment slot="solution">
<IdeaTabs labels={["Cross-entropy surrogate", "Localized EM"]}>
  <Fragment slot="tab-0">
    <EqBox label="Proposition 4.3 — Cross-entropy surrogate">
      <div class="md:flex md:items-center md:gap-5">
        <div class="min-w-0 md:basis-[63%]">

        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        D_{\mathrm{TV}}(\pi, \tilde\pi) &= \mathcal{O}\big(\sqrt{\mathrm{tr}(\Sigma)}\big), \quad
        D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\pi \,\|\, \tilde\pi) = \mathcal{O}\big(\mathrm{tr}(\Sigma)^2\big) \\[4pt]
        \mathcal{H}(\pi, \tilde\pi) &= \mathcal{H}(\pi) + \mathcal{O}\big(\mathrm{tr}(\Sigma)^2\big) \approx \mathcal{H}(\pi)
        \end{aligned}
        $$

        </div>
        <div class="mt-3 min-w-0 md:mt-0 md:basis-[37%]">
          <Picture src="../assets/Proposition_4_3.pdf" alt="Plot showing the global policy converging to the base flow as the local variance shrinks" />
        </div>
      </div>
    </EqBox>

    - Replace the entropy $\mathcal{H}(\pi)$ with the **cross-entropy** $\mathcal{H}(\pi, \tilde\pi)$ against the base flow $\tilde\pi$.
    - Estimate $\log \tilde \pi(a\mid s)$ via the **Hutchinson trace estimator** — Use **common random numbers (CRN)** to reduce variance in the estimator (Appendix D.3).
  </Fragment>
  <Fragment slot="tab-1">
    <EqBox label="Localized EM on the z-MDP">
      <div class="md:flex md:items-center md:gap-5">
        <div class="min-w-0 md:basis-[63%]">

        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \textbf{E-step:}\;\; & q_k(a \mid \hat s) \propto \hat\pi(a \mid \hat s; \theta_k)\,\exp\!\big(f_{\hat s, k}(a)/\lambda\big) \\[6pt]
        \textbf{M-step:}\;\; & \theta_{k+1} \in \operatorname*{arg\,min}_{\theta}\; \mathbb{E}_{(s,z)\sim\hat\rho_{\hat\pi}}\big\|\, T_\theta(s,z) - \mu_k^*(\hat s) \big\|^2 \\[2pt]
        & \mu_k^*(\hat s) := \mathbb{E}_{q_k}[a]
        \end{aligned}
        $$

        </div>
        <div class="mt-3 min-w-0 md:mt-0 md:basis-[37%]">
          <Picture src={localizedEM} alt="Per-latent local Gaussians are shifted toward the Q-weighted target within their local region and distilled back into the flow via CFM" />
        </div>
      </div>
    </EqBox>

    - **E-step** — a local Boltzmann tilt of the Gaussian proposal around each anchor.
    - **M-step** — supervised regression of the flow onto improved action labels $\mu_k^*$, using *Method of moments* to estimate the labels with $N$ local samples.
  </Fragment>
</IdeaTabs>
</Fragment>

<Fragment slot="bottom-line">
We use the **cross-entropy as a tractable entropy surrogate** and update the flow via an **EM algorithm on the z-MDP** — supervised distillation onto locally improved labels, *not* gradient-through-flow.
</Fragment>

</QCard>

<QCard number="Q3" title="Does FLAG provably improve the policy, and how does it relate to SAC?">

<Fragment slot="challenge">
FLAG updates the policy by **supervised distillation**, not by differentiating the objective through the flow. Two guarantees are therefore not obvious — and this section establishes both:

- **Monotonic improvement.** Does FLAG's update actually raise the objective, i.e. is $\mathcal{J}_{k+1} \geq \mathcal{J}_k$ guaranteed?
- **Relation to SAC.** We optimize a MaxEnt-RL objective — so how does FLAG's update relate to **Soft Actor–Critic** (SAC), and does it inherit SAC's *soft policy improvement*?
</Fragment>

<Fragment slot="solution">
<IdeaTabs labels={["Monotonic Improvement", "Relation to SAC"]}>
  <Fragment slot="tab-0">
    <EqBox label="Theorem 4.5 — Monotonic Improvement Guarantee">

    $$
    \mathcal{J}_{k+1} - \mathcal{J}_k
    \;\geq\;
    \underbrace{\lambda \beta \mathcal{G}_k}_{\substack{\text{frozen-reward}\\\text{MPO improvement}}}
    \;-\;
    \underbrace{\alpha\, C_\Sigma\, \sigma_k^2\, \beta \mathcal{G}_k}_{\substack{\text{cross-entropy}\\\text{reward drift}}}
    \;-\;
    \underbrace{\lambda\, \epsilon_k^{\mathrm{proj}}}_{\substack{\text{CFM projection}\\\text{error}}}
    \;+\; \mathcal{O}(\beta^2)
    $$

    </EqBox>

    - One FLAG step splits into a **positive MPO improvement** term, a **cross-entropy drift** that scales with the local variance $\sigma_k^2$, and the **CFM projection error** $\epsilon_k^{\mathrm{proj}}$.
    - Whenever $\lambda > \alpha\, C_\Sigma\, \sigma_k^2$ and the projection error is small, the improvement term dominates, so $\mathcal{J}_{k+1} \geq \mathcal{J}_k$ — **monotonic improvement**.

    <ProofRoadmap src={mpoProofRoadmap} alt="Proof roadmap for the MPO-style monotonic improvement of FLAG" />
  </Fragment>
  <Fragment slot="tab-1">
    <div class="not-prose space-y-3">
      <EqBox label="SAC — Soft policy improvement">

      $$
      \pi_{\mathrm{new}} = \operatorname*{arg\,min}_{\pi \in \Pi} D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\left( \pi(a \mid s) \,\Big\|\, \tfrac{1}{Z}\exp\!\big(Q^{\pi_{\mathrm{old}}}/\alpha\big) \right)
      \;\Longrightarrow\;
      Q^{\pi_{\mathrm{new}}} \geq Q^{\pi_{\mathrm{old}}}
      $$

      </EqBox>
      <EqBox label="FLAG — zeroth-order realization">

      $$
      \begin{aligned}
      g_k(\mu) &:= \underbrace{\log \mathbb{E}_{\delta \sim \mathcal{N}(0,\, \Sigma_k)}\big[\exp\!\big( f_{\hat s, k}(\mu + \delta)/\lambda \big)\big]}_{\text{log-sum-exp smoothing}} \\[12pt]
      \underbrace{\mu_k^*(\hat s) - \mu_k}_{\text{FLAG M-step}} &\overset{(\text{Stein's identity})}{=} \Sigma_k \nabla_\mu g_k
      \;\approx\;
      \Sigma_k \underbrace{\nabla_a\!\big( Q^{\tilde\pi}(s, a) - \alpha \log \tilde\pi_{\theta_k}(a \mid s) \big)}_{\text{SAC action gradient}}
      \end{aligned}
      $$

      </EqBox>
    </div>

    - SAC's soft policy improvement theorem is **agnostic to how** the KL minimizer is obtained.
    - **SAC** restricts $\Pi$ to a Gaussian and takes a *first-order* reparameterization gradient; **FLAG** solves the same KL minimization with a *non-parametric, action-level* update — a **zeroth-order** approximation via Stein's identity and log-sum-exp smoothing.
    <small class="mt-2 block text-xs italic text-zinc-500 dark:text-zinc-400">Full statements and details are in Appendix C.2.</small>

    <ProofRoadmap src={sacProofRoadmap} alt="Proof roadmap comparing SAC's reparameterized update with FLAG's non-parametric EM update" />
  </Fragment>
</IdeaTabs>
</Fragment>

<Fragment slot="bottom-line">
Monotonic improvement is established from **two complementary perspectives — MPO (variational) and SAC (soft policy improvement)** — and FLAG realizes SAC-style improvement for expressive flow policies **without ever differentiating through the flow ODE**.
</Fragment>

</QCard>

#### Hutchinson variance reduction

FLAG estimates $\log\tilde\pi$ with the Hutchinson trace estimator, sharing random tangent vectors across action samples (CRN). This both **accelerates learning** and **sharply reduces variance across seeds**, yielding more robust convergence.

#### Cross-entropy surrogate

Replacing the intractable entropy with the cross-entropy surrogate lets FLAG reach 90% of peak performance **≈10% faster on Dog-run and ≈41% faster on Dog-trot** than a no-cross-entropy variant, by regularizing against early value over-estimation.

#### Trust-region temperature $\lambda_{\text{ref}}$
